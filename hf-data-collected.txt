Why

discover() (src/seismo/collectors/hf.py:72) intersects two sets that barely overlap:

1. Trending right now — it pages sort=trendingScore for 20 pages (2,000 models, MAX_PAGES:49).
2. Born inside the window — typically the last 24h (hf.py:103).

The trending list is old. Age percentiles from the live scan: p50 = 86 days, p75 = 242, p90 = 595. Only 9 of 2,000 trending models were born in the last day — 0.45%.

Then two more gates cut that 9 down:

- Traction floor (hf.py:47): downloads >= 1000 OR likes >= 10. Of the 9 newborns, exactly 1 clears it. This is the killer — a model born yesterday hasn't accumulated 1,000 downloads yet, by definition.
- Relevance (hf.py:31): the single survivor was FINAL-Bench/POCKET-Image-Zimage, pipeline_tag=text-to-image — not in RELEVANT_PIPELINES, no keyword hit. Rejected. → 0 kept.

The docstring at hf.py:73-80 actually names this tension ("sorting by createdAt is self-defeating — the newest models have the least usage") and tries to solve it by scanning trending instead. But that just inverts the same problem: requiring a model to be both newborn and already-trending and already-high-traction is a near-empty set.

The cleanest fix is a two-pass discovery: keep the trending pass as-is for quality, and add a second pass sorted by createdAt desc that admits young models provisionally with a low bar, letting the existing track() pass promote them once downloads actually accumulate. That matches how the maturity ladder already works and stops requiring a model to be famous on day one.

One caveat you should weigh: occurred_at is stamped with the model's real createdAt (hf.py:104), so widening the window back-dates events into already-processed history, which can disturb momentum states for past days. That is very likely why the tight window is there. A provisional-admission pass avoids this by stamping discovery at observation time instead.

Which do you want? I'd start with widening the window to 7–14 days plus broadening the pipeline set — smallest change, ~80×  the yield — and treat the two-pass redesign as a follow-up. But the back-dating question is a real design call on your system, so tell me how you want to handle it before I touch the collector.
