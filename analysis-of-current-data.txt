Part 2 — The asset you're underusing (this is the key to your PM/VC vision)

You asked whether you can analyze "impact on different industries if a project/patent/idea achieves bigger results, and how it can impact stock markets."

The answer is yes for a qualitative, evidence-linked disruption engine — and the data for it already exists but you're only using it in one direction.

Right now you use the map entity → company. But your exposure_companies.doc is far richer than the brief pipeline uses. Here's the real NVDA doc I pulled:

NVDA · datacenter (88% of revenue)
  threat_surface:
    - model-efficiency   → demand_risk   (core: true)   ← a category!
    - inference-runtime  → demand_risk   (core: false)
    - ml-compiler        → dependency_risk
    - distributed-systems→ enablement

Every company already declares which technology categories threaten or enable each revenue line, weighted by share_of_revenue and a core flag. And you have live momentum for every entity in those categories. That means you can reverse the arrow and go company → threatened revenue lines → categories → live momentum — which is exactly the PM/VC tool you're describing. You don't need new data to build it; you need to query the map backwards.

Part 3 — Features you can build, ranked by data-readiness

✅ Ready NOW (data fully supports it)

1. Company Disruption-Pressure Index (the flagship PM feature)
For each of the 30 tickers: for every revenue line, sum the momentum of all live entities in that line's threat_surface categories, weighted by share_of_revenue × core. Split by relation:
- substitution_partial / demand_risk → headwind
- enablement / dependency_risk → tailwind

Output: "NVDA datacenter (88% of revenue) faces 12 accelerating + 2 breakout model-efficiency entities this week (core threat) → headwind score rising 3 weeks straight." This is a single number per company, per week, fully back-testable. Everything needed is in exposure_companies.doc + momentum_states + entity_category_history.

2. Sector / Category Heat Indices
Aggregate momentum_states by category over time → "which tech categories are heating." You have 786k daily readings back to January. Gives PMs a "where is capital-attracting activity accelerating" line chart per category. Pure aggregation, zero new data.

3. Cohort Base-Rate / Survival Analytics
The momentum inputs JSONB already stores cohort_key, cohort_n, percentile counts. With 6 months of history you can answer "what % of agent-framework breakouts are still ≥accelerating 30 days later?" — turning every brief's confidence from a vibe into a calibrated survival probability. This is the number a PM actually trusts. Feasible today from historical momentum_states.

4. Portfolio / Watchlist Threat Alerts
A PM uploads their holdings (subset of your 30 tickers). You map each to its threat_surface categories and alert when a matching entity crosses into breakout. Reuses feature #1's join.

5. Two-Sided Exposure View (headwind vs tailwind)
Your reach_links.relation already distinguishes enablement, substitution_partial, demand_risk, dependency_risk. Present each company as a net tailwind/headwind bar rather than one-directional. Field already exists.

🟡 Ready after ONE fix you've already identified

6. Calibrated leading-indicator backtest — needs brief_scores populated (currently 0 rows) and hindcast_runs run (currently 0). You have the immutable history to do it; it's wiring, not new data. This is what lets you claim "our breakout signal preceded the move."

7. Fix the categoriser first — 4,023 entities are uncategorized, and I pulled real current breakouts that are uncategorized: rust-lang/rust, oven-sh/bun, unclecode/crawl4ai, jamiepine/voicebox. Uncategorized → no reach → invisible to every feature above. This is the single biggest multiplier on all of Part 3.

🔴 Needs a NEW data source (be honest about the ceiling)

8. Quantitative price/return impact — Your ambition of "how it impacts stock markets" has a hard limit: there is no market price, returns, or fundamentals data anywhere in the DB. Today you can produce directional, falsifiable, evidence-linked theses (headwind/tailwind + horizon) — genuinely valuable to a PM as a research signal — but you cannot yet correlate momentum → actual stock returns or claim quantitative alpha. To cross that line you'd add one market_prices table (daily OHLCV for the 30 tickers) and join it to your momentum indices. That's the difference between "here's a disruption signal to investigate" and "this signal has historically preceded a 4% move."
